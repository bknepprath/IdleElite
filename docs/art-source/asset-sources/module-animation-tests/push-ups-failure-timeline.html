Push-Ups Animation Failure Timeline

This page tracks the failed Push-Ups animation attempts in order so future passes can target the actual problems instead of repeating near-misses. The hard visual target is the source-of-truth blue guy doing a readable push-up: two visible legs, planted hands and toe pads, fixed guide only in diagnostics, and no body-to-leg seam.

No body-to-leg seam No black hip crease, butt seam, underside divider, or fat line below the body and above the legs.
Two visible legs Do not solve the seam by creating a single merged unileg or tail. Show two simple legs that attach smoothly.
No bouncing guide The diagnostic floor line is a fixed world reference added after slicing. It must not be generated per frame.
Stable contact patches The visible hand pads and toe pads must stay planted, not just one hidden anchor pixel.
Rejected

1. Smooth Rear Pass

push-ups-smooth-rear

  • Good: smoother rear shape, closer to the requested blue-guy feel.
  • Wrong: feet floated and jumped frame to frame instead of rotating from planted toes.
  • Wrong: no real floor/contact plane was being obeyed.
Lesson: good silhouette is not enough. Push-ups need a physical contact plane before polish.
Rejected

2. Generated Red-Line Guide Passes

push-ups-guided-v1 through push-ups-guided-v4

  • Good: introduced a visible construction line to reason about the floor.
  • Wrong: the red line itself moved, bent, or was reinterpreted per frame.
  • Wrong: the character did not actually obey the line; it was just a ruler behind floating art.
Lesson: diagnostic guides must be code-authored fixed overlays, not hallucinated animation elements.
Rejected

3. Anchor-Locked Export

push-ups-floor-authored-v2

  • Good: tried to align the generated frames using contact anchors after slicing.
  • Wrong: the chosen anchor pixels were steadier, but the visible foot mass still crawled.
  • Wrong: the viewer reads the whole toe/foot pad, not a mathematical point.
Lesson: lock the visible contact patch, not just an invisible point.
Rejected

4. Contact Dot Pass

push-ups-contact-dots-v1

  • Good: red contact dots made the feet and hands more physically anchored.
  • Wrong: the guide/dots read as animated marks and could appear to bounce.
  • Wrong: still did not fully solve the lower-body shape.
Lesson: source guide dots can help generation, but the review guide must still be a fixed post-process overlay.
Rejected

5. Fused Plank Pass

push-ups-fused-plank-v1

  • Good: reduced the black butt/body-to-leg seam.
  • Wrong: overcorrected into a single unileg/tail lower body.
  • Wrong: the lower silhouette became awkward instead of reading as two blue-guy legs.
Lesson: the fix is not one fused tail. The correct target is two legs with a smooth body attachment.
Rejected

6. Two-Leg Pass

push-ups-two-leg-v1

  • Good: restored the two-leg read.
  • Wrong: added a fat black underside divider below the body and above the legs.
  • Wrong: that line is still a forbidden body-to-leg seam, even if it is not drawn as a round butt outline.
Lesson: allow only a subtle line between the two legs. Never allow a transverse underside line separating torso/body from legs.
Rejected

7. Painted Underside Cleanup

push-ups-no-underside-v1

  • Good: directly removed the forbidden fat underside line in several frames.
  • Wrong: the paintover left obvious blue bands and damaged the art quality.
  • Wrong: cleanup solved the rule technically but looked patched instead of authored.
Lesson: manual cleanup can help, but broad paint strokes are not acceptable. Any cleanup must preserve the original shading and contour quality.
Rejected

8. Hidden Upper-Leg Prompt

push-ups-hidden-upper-leg-v1

  • Good: tried a new structure where upper legs are mostly hidden and two toe pads give the leg read.
  • Wrong: low frames still reintroduced the black underside/body-to-leg divider.
  • Wrong: prompt-only generation keeps using that black stroke to explain overlap in low push-up poses.
Lesson: the next real strategy should use either a cleaner pose reference with the lower body already solved, or a precise edit pass on only the seam with source-aware blending.
Closest

9. Two-Leg Stable Body Pass

push-ups-two-leg-stable-body-v1

  • Good: restores the two-leg/toe-pad read without returning to a full unileg shape.
  • Good: reduces the pronounced butt and belly compared with earlier two-leg attempts.
  • Better: torso length feels more stable, with less hip/butt crawling along the body.
  • Still wrong: low frames retain a small underside crease/body-to-leg divider, so it is not final.
Lesson: this is the current best direction. Next pass should preserve this body mass and two-leg read while removing the remaining underside crease in the low frames.
Rejected

10. Two-Leg No-Ass Prompt

push-ups-two-leg-no-ass-v1

  • Good: made the rear legs more readable than the one-leg attempts.
  • Wrong: still showed rear/belly mass, especially in the higher frames.
  • Wrong: feet still did not feel fully stamped to one fixed contact patch in motion.
Lesson: asking for no ass is not enough; the construction must avoid tucking the legs under a belly/rear mass.
Closest

11. Two-Leg Outside Pass

push-ups-two-leg-outside-v1

  • Good: clearest two-leg read so far; legs are more visible outside the body silhouette.
  • Better: foot shapes are easier to track and less one-leg/tail-like.
  • Still wrong: low frames retain a dark body-to-leg divider, and the torso still has some rear/belly mass.
Lesson: this is a better structural direction, but the low-pose body/leg overlap still needs a no-divider solution.
Rejected

12. Slim Plank Prompt

push-ups-slim-plank-v1

  • Good: tried to reduce the fat body by specifying a slim rounded-rectangle plank.
  • Wrong: low frames still created a fat body mass with a black line separating body from legs.
  • Wrong: feet still bounced and the legs appeared to extend in length during the animation.
Lesson: full-sheet image generation keeps changing the topology in the down pose. The next production strategy should animate consistent generated parts or a rigged single-source drawing rather than asking for a new complete 4-frame sheet.
Best Source

13. Exercise Source Rig

push-ups-exercise-source-rig-v2

  • Good: built from the actual exercise sprite sheet push-up poses instead of asking image generation to redraw topology.
  • Good: removes the fat generated torso, ass read, black body-to-leg seam, and leg-extension problem.
  • Good: two legs/toe pads come from the source-of-truth exercise pose.
  • Tradeoff: in-between motion is less fluid because frames 2 and 3 are near-key source-pose holds, not fully redrawn tweens.
Lesson: the source sheet solved the style/topology problem. Future polish should rig or redraw tweens from this source, not generate a new full sheet.
Current Best

14. Exercise Foot-Anchor Tweens

push-ups-exercise-foot-anchor-v1

  • Good: uses the exercise source push-up poses and keeps the same foot anchor across all exported frames.
  • Good: adds explicit near-key tweens: frame 2 is the 10% near-high pose, frame 3 is the 90% near-low pose.
  • Good: diagnostic red dot/line are code-authored and do not vertically bounce.
  • Tradeoff: tweens are transformed from source poses, so future polish can still redraw smoother in-betweens from this anchored base.
Lesson: anchor-first export fixes the foot bounce problem better than full-sheet generation. Keep this anchor workflow for the next polish pass.
Source Locked

15. Exercise Source-Locked V2

push-ups-exercise-source-locked-v2

  • Good: stops prompt-only redraws from inventing the tall blue guy and uses the exercise sheet push-up topology directly.
  • Good: keeps the compact exercise-sheet style, no fat generated body, no pronounced ass, and no black body-to-leg cutoff line.
  • Good: exports four unique ordered frames with a shared crop, fixed diagnostic floor overlay, and longer top-hold timing.
  • Tradeoff: this is a source-locked rig/export pass, not a fully fresh image-gen redraw; future polish should redraw tweens from this topology only after the source lock is preserved.
Lesson: for this animation, source-sheet topology wins over prompt-only generation. Image gen can polish later, but it should not be allowed to reinvent the body/leg structure.
Best Image Gen

16. Image-Generated Continuous Body

push-ups-imagegen-continuous-body-v1

  • Good: true image-generated sheet with four unique poses: 0%, 15%, 85%, and 100%.
  • Good: uses the exercise-sheet push-up orientation with feet on the left and head on the right.
  • Good: shared export scale and fixed baseline keep the toe/hand contact plane stable in the preview.
  • Improved: the long black body-to-leg separator from earlier generated passes is mostly gone; the lower contour reads closer to an outer silhouette.
  • Watch: low frames still have a heavy lower outline, so reject future regressions that turn that contour back into a body-over-leg divider.
Lesson: prompt generation only became usable after targeting one failure at a time, then packaging with shared-scale slicing and a fixed diagnostic overlay.
Current Best

17. Image-Generated Hand-Lock V3

push-ups-imagegen-handlock-v3

  • Good: keeps the strong feet-locked behavior from the previous image-generated candidate.
  • Better: hand contact patches stay much closer to the fixed support point during the lowering motion.
  • Better: reduces the raised mid-back/hip bump while keeping the body-to-leg connection continuous.
  • Watch: palms still change silhouette slightly as elbows bend, so future polish should keep using fixed hand dots during generation/review.
Lesson: once feet are solved, palms must become first-class anchors. The body should lower by elbow bend around planted hands, not by sliding the hands along the floor.
